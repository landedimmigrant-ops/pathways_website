Defining research impact

At Concordia, research impact is the change that research activities contribute to society and beyond — an ongoing process of engagement that can lead to academic, social, cultural, economic, environmental, or policy effects.

Learn more about research impact →
The shape of impact

Impact is not a single kind of thing, and it does not always arrive quickly. Knowing which kind you are working toward, and over what horizon, is where planning starts.

The challenge

Research doesn’t automatically become impact. Knowledge moves through systems — academic, social, political — and the path is rarely straight. Most researchers navigate it without a map.

Zone 3 — the tour, as a toy. Each of the six beats is one wide slide, sliced into three vertical strips. Each strip is its own reel, so mid-spin you get a chimera — one beat's title beside another's artwork. The reels always land together on the same beat, so it always resolves into one slide that reads properly.

What it costs. Chance is fun but it removes choice, which is the wrong trade for a researcher who arrived with a question. The six buttons underneath are the real navigation; the reels are the flourish. They are hidden from screen readers, the result is announced, and the prose under the machine is ordinary readable content rather than something you have to win.

That is what Pathways is. The site itself starts here.
