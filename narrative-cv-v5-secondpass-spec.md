# Narrative CV V5 — second-pass copy spec

**What this is.** The copy for the items Prem's second pass (2026-09-11) sent back for rebuild, plus
the two worked examples B-37 asks for. Companion to
[narrative-cv-v5-copy-spec.md](narrative-cv-v5-copy-spec.md) (the 191-string first pass — Section A's
six-job vocabulary and Section B's exemplar treatment still stand) and
[narrative-cv-v5-decisions.md](narrative-cv-v5-decisions.md) (the **Overrides** table is the brief
for everything here).

**Numbering.** New and replacement strings run **S192–S241**, continuing the first spec. Where a
string replaces one of S1–S191 the old number is named. Nothing here renumbers anything.

**How to read an entry.** *Now* — the string in `narrative-cv-prototype-v5.html`, verbatim, with its
line. *S-number* — the replacement, to be copied exactly. *Why* — one line, from the researcher's
side.

**Privacy — the ship gate.** Every person, institution, project, partner, destination and number in
the two new worked examples is invented for this document. No real researcher, trainee, institution
or project appears, and the fictional people in the existing Contributions exemplars are not reused.
Institutions are deliberately left unnamed ("a mid-sized Canadian university", "a provincial
ministry") rather than given invented proper names, so nothing can be mistaken for a real Concordia
researcher.

**House rules applied throughout** (`pathways-voice`): one audience — a researcher; second person;
questions where the reader is being asked something; condition first; no Oxford comma; Canadian
spelling; zero to nine spelled out, figures from 10. *Must* only where a funder says must. Reader-
facing word for the six is **job**. Kept names: **Structural read**, **Tri-agency CV**, **CV-FRQ**.

# Section 1 · The intro paragraph (B-1 + B-4)

## S192 · Setup stage lede

- **Now** (938) — "By the end you will have a draft in your own words — your contributions, your
  mentorship record and a personal statement — plus the questions you want to take to an advisor.
  Nothing you type leaves this browser."
- **S192** —

> This tool will help you get familiar with how a narrative CV asks you to think about your work and
> describe it. It is a prototype, and it works in English only for now. It runs without AI models —
> the checks are fixed rules, and nothing you type leaves this browser. What you build here is a
> first outline in your own words: your contributions, your mentorship record and a personal
> statement, plus the questions you want to take to an advisor.

**Rationale.** The override puts the tool's intention ahead of its output, so the first sentence now
answers "what is this for" and the last answers "what do I leave with" — the two questions a
researcher asks in that order. The prototype, English-only and no-AI facts sit between them because
each one changes how much weight a reader gives everything that follows, and a person who learns
later that this is a prototype trusts the earlier screens less, not more.

**Length note.** At 83 words this runs past the house paragraph target. It carries five separate
claims that the override says must appear together and in this order; splitting it into two
paragraphs would put the privacy claim below the mode cards, where B-38 established it does not get
read.

## S193 · Page subtitle — yes, it now duplicates

- **Now** (163) — "Start fresh, or get a structural read of an existing draft. Guided prompts, live
  checks, and an advisor packet."
- **Duplication check** — it duplicates twice over. Sentence one is the mode cards' own question,

- **S193** — "A drafting space for the Tri-agency CV and the CV-FRQ."

# Section 2 · Personal statement worked example (B-37, new)

**Discipline: social sciences.** Stated in the box title. Chosen because the default Contributions
exemplar a health researcher sees is health, because the personal statement is the least
discipline-shaped section in the format — all five disciplines answer the same six prompts — and
because a social-science record shows both empirical scale and public uptake, so a STEM reader and a
humanities reader can each see their own version of the moves.

**One variant, not five.** Producing five would be disproportionate for a researcher test in the next
few days, and the personal statement is where a single example transfers best: what the reader is
learning is the *sequence* of moves, not the vocabulary of a field. Recommended follow-up if the
fixed example reads oddly beside a switching Contributions exemplar: add a STEM and a creative
variant after the researcher session, keyed to `ns.lens.discipline` exactly as `EXEMPLARS` is.

**Deliberately not the fictional people in `EXEMPLARS`.** This is a fresh invented researcher, who
also appears in the mentorship example in Section 3 — one invented record, so a reader who meets both
boxes does not meet three fictional strangers.

## S194 · The full prose

> I am an associate professor at a mid-sized Canadian university. My work is in the sociology of work
> and economic insecurity.
>
> For 15 years my research has come back to one question: why does unpredictable scheduling fall
> hardest on the workers least able to absorb it?
>
> Three strands answer it — an ethnography of how shifts get assigned in retail and warehousing, a
> repeated survey of household income volatility and a partnership with a workers' centre testing
> what changes when schedules are posted two weeks ahead. The thread is the same in all three: the
> cost of uncertainty is carried by the household, not the firm.
>

> school. It is why I start from what a rule does in a workplace, not what it says on paper.

> research teams now use. A provincial ministry reviewing employment standards has asked for my
> analysis twice, and the workers' centre has kept the partnership running for seven years.
>

> this work has been built since 2019, and sustained partnership is where my record is strongest.
>
> The next phase follows the same households into care work, where scheduling volatility is growing
> fastest and almost no longitudinal data exists.

# Section 5 · The three mentorship questions (B-30 / B-31)

Rule applied to all three: **ask for one specific instance, never a category**, and never a shape a
canned statement will fit.

## Q1 · The practice — keep the question, sharpen the help

**S213 · Question** — unchanged from S147:

> What is one concrete thing you do that shows how you mentor?

**S214 · Help**

- **Now** (602) — "Not a slogan — a practice. How you run a first meeting, how you decide
  authorship, how you hand a project over when someone is ready for it."
- **S214** — "Not a slogan — one practice you actually have. How you run a first meeting, how you
  decide authorship, how you hand a project over when someone is ready for it."

**Rationale.** The question already asks for one instance and already says *concrete*; the only gap
was that "a practice" can still be read as a policy you would describe in general, so the help now
says *one practice you actually have*.

## Q2 · Access — drop the label, keep the question

**S215 · Question**

- **Now** (605) — "Equity in practice — who gets into your group, and what have you changed about how
  they get there?"
- **S215** — "Who gets into your group, and what have you changed about how they get there?"

**S216 · Help**

- **Now** (605) — "This asks for a structure, not a statement: a recruitment route you changed, a
  program you built, a barrier in your environment you work against. Reviewers are asked to look at
  equity in the research process itself, so one concrete practice reads better than a position."
- **S216** — "One thing you changed, not a statement of values: a posting you rewrote, a requirement
  you dropped, a route you opened or a barrier in your environment you work against. Reviewers are
  asked to look at equity in the research process itself, so what you changed carries further than
  what you believe."

**S217 · Community variant** — unchanged from S151:

> Who do your community partnerships bring into research, and what did you change to make that
> possible?

**Rationale.** The abstract noun in the label was doing the summoning: a field that opens with
"Equity in practice" is a field a researcher fills with an equity statement, whatever the question
underneath asks. Stripped of the label, the question is self-evidently about access and it asks for
a change with a date behind it — and the help now names four things that are all *acts*, so the
reader has nowhere to put a position.

## Q3 · Beyond your own students — ask for the practice too

**S218 · Question**

- **Now** (607) — "Beyond your own students — who else do you mentor?"
- **S218** — "Beyond your own students — who else do you mentor? Take one person and say what you
  actually did."

**S219 · Help**

- **Now** (607) — "Informal mentoring counts here: early-career colleagues, postdocs you don't
  supervise, visiting scholars, peers, community researchers."
- **S219** — "Informal mentoring counts here, and the format says so: early-career colleagues,
  postdocs you don't supervise, visiting scholars, peers, community researchers. One example with
  something in it — a grant you read, an introduction you made, a room you got someone into — says
  more than the list of who."

**Rationale.** A question ending in "who else do you mentor?" gets four nouns and no evidence, which
is the weakest thing on the stage; the second sentence turns it into the same ask as Q1 and Q2 — one
person, one thing you did. Second, the funder fact belongs here: the Tri-agency CV counts informal
mentorship explicitly, so a researcher without graduate students has an answer.

## B-7 · The "Edit context" control

**S220 · First choice** — collapsed "Change these answers" · expanded "Hide these answers"

**S221 · Alternative** — collapsed "Change what this is tailored for" · expanded "Hide"

# Open items

1. **Discipline label on the two new boxes.** They are fixed at social sciences while the
   Contributions exemplar switches with `ns.lens.discipline`. Naming the discipline in the title is
   the honest option and it is what S197 and S204 do, but a STEM researcher will see one box that
   matches their discipline and two that announce they do not. The alternative — dropping "— Social
   sciences" from both titles — hides the mismatch rather than fixing it. Recommend shipping with the
   labels and deciding on variants after the researcher session.
2. **Guide anchors** (Section 7 flag). Needs the guide rewrite, or five minutes of anchor ids.
3. **Q2's help still uses the word "equity"**, in the sentence sourcing the reviewer criterion. The
   override drops the *label*, and a reader who never sees the word will not think about the thing;
   this is the one place it earns its keep. Flagging it in case the second pass meant the word
   everywhere.
