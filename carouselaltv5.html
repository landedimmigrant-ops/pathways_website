What Pathways to Impact is

Zone 3 — the tour, one set of markup. This is a six-item accordion in the HTML: a heading button, then a panel holding the figure and the copy. That is what phones and tablets get, what a screen reader gets, and what renders with JavaScript off (every panel open). At 900px and up a script adds one class and moves the six figures into the shared sticky stage, and the accordion becomes v1's scroll narrative. Resize the window across 900px to watch it switch. For AEM this means one authored Accordion component, with the desktop treatment as a client library on top.

What's new versus v1 + v3. (1) A Next link at the foot of every open panel, so the sequence survives on a phone where there is no scroll to carry it. (2) Opening a lower beat scrolls its heading to the top, so the panel is never off-screen. (3) The progress ticks on the desktop stage are buttons: click one to jump to that beat. (4) Each beat has a URL, #beat-4 opens or scrolls to the fourth. (5) Tablets keep the accordion but lay copy beside the figure. (6) No step numbers on the beats: these are sections a reader can take in any order, not slides in a deck.

Scroll rhythm. A beat is exactly as tall as the graphic card, so one card-height of scroll advances one beat and the empty run-up between beats is gone. Each beat also snaps to the centre of the window as you slow down, which is why a scroll settles with the words and the picture on the same line. The snap is by proximity, so a reader who keeps going is never held. Reduced-motion visitors get no snap and no fades.

Authoring, for the web team. The progress ticks and the Next links are generated from the beats at run time, taking their labels from the headings, so a seventh beat is one more authored accordion item and nothing else. Each beat carries a data-beat attribute, which is the hook the script attaches to.

  1. What do we mean by change?

    At Concordia, research impact is the change that research activities contribute to society and beyond — an ongoing process of engagement that can lead to academic, social, cultural, economic, environmental, or policy effects.

    Learn more about research impact →

  2. Impact takes more than one shape.

    Impact can be…
    Positive or negative
    Different types & natures
    Different times & scales
    From the process & its results

    Impact is not a single kind of thing, and it does not always arrive quickly. Knowing which kind you are working toward, and over what horizon, is where planning starts.

  3. The gap between knowledge and change is real.

    Research doesn’t automatically become impact. Knowledge moves through systems — academic, social, political — and the path is rarely straight. Most researchers navigate it without a map.
