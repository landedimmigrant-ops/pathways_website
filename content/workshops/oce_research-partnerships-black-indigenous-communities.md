This interactive workshop supports researchers who want practical guidance and frameworks for partnering with Montréal's Black and Indigenous communities in research design and practice. It highlights how these communities are shaping the transformation of research, and helps researchers approach engagement with clarity, intention, and respect.

Community-engaged research strengthens impact by shaping research questions, methods, and relationships from the start — not only how findings are shared at the end. Strong partnerships improve relevance, trust, and uptake beyond academia, and can support stronger research design and funding applications.

## What you'll get

- A clearer understanding of the historical context shaping university–community collaborations
- Stronger awareness of Indigenous and Black perspectives on community engagement
- Greater clarity on what reciprocal, meaningful, and collaborative research looks like
- Practical ways to approach intentional relationship-building with community partners
- Reflection prompts to support decision-making around intention, role, and responsibilities

## What to bring

- A research proposal (draft or in progress), and/or
- Reflections from past research partnership experiences

## Who it's for

Researchers at any career stage who are developing or planning research with Indigenous and/or Black community partners; revising research design to better reflect community priorities; preparing partnership-based funding applications; or looking to deepen their understanding of responsibilities and relationship-building in community-engaged research.

**Presenters:** Geneviève Sioui (Coordinator, Indigenous Community Engagement) and Kristen Young (Coordinator, Black Community Engagement), Office of Community Engagement & SHIFT, Concordia University. In-person at the SHIFT Centre for Social Transformation, SGW campus. Offered twice per year — check LibCal for upcoming dates.
