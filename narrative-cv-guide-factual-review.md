# Narrative CV guide — factual review

**For: Eli.** Fourteen content questions in `content/learn/narrative-cv-guide.md`, found during a
voice pass on 2026-08-10. **Nothing here has been applied.** The voice version
(`content/learn/narrative-cv-guide-pathways-voice.md`) deliberately carries every one of these
claims across unchanged, so the voice work can be reviewed without also signing off on funder facts.

**Where the sourcing comes from.** Mostly `ncv-genre-sources.md` in this repo — a sourced scan of
the TCV, CV-FRQ and international formats with URLs and dates — plus Concordia's own published NCV
page and FAQ. That document has its own caveats in its §6; anything it flags as unverified is
flagged here too. **I have not gone back to the agencies to confirm any of this.** Three items
(F-2, F-5, F-10) need someone who actually knows (F-2 answered 2026-09-22), not more desk research.

**How to read the confidence column.** *High* = stated explicitly in a primary source I can point
to. *Medium* = sourced but with a caveat. *Unverified* = I could not confirm the current text and
could not confirm a replacement either.

## High priority

### F-1 · The guide implies funders require the first person

**Current** (`s4.expand-a.p1`):
> "Yes — and it is not just permitted, it is the point. Funders want to understand your individual
> contribution to collaborative work. Using 'we' throughout makes it impossible for reviewers to
> assess your specific role."

**Issue.** `ncv-genre-sources.md` §1 records this as explicitly checked: *no agency mandates first
person* — verified across CIHR, SSHRC and NSERC instructions, the FAQ and the reviewer guidelines.
What **is** mandated is that your role is unambiguous in every contribution. First-person advice is
institutional, coming from Concordia, U Winnipeg and Aberdeen rather than from the agencies.

Two problems follow. The guide attributes a preference to funders they haven't expressed, and — more
importantly — it never states the requirement that *does* exist. A researcher could write flawless
first-person prose and still fail the actual test if their role stays vague.

**Proposed:**
> "Yes. No agency actually requires the first person — what the Tri-agency requires is that your
> role in each contribution is unambiguous. First person is the most reliable way to achieve that,
> which is why Concordia recommends it. If every sentence says 'we', reviewers cannot tell what you
> did."

**Confidence: High.** The source records this as verified across all three agencies' instructions.

### F-2 · "French required for most programs" (CV-FRQ)

**Current** (`s3.table`, Language row): `English or French | French required for most programs`

**Issue.** `ncv-genre-sources.md` §2 records the opposite: FRQ *forms* are in French, but **attached
documents may be French or English** — an anglophone can file an English CV-FRQ (5 pp) inside a
French form. It cites the presentation-standards PDF.

**But the same document's §6 flags the underlying rule (RGC art. 3.6) as not directly verified.**

**This is the one I would not change on my own authority.** If the guide is right and we "correct"
it, an anglophone researcher submits in English to a competition that requires French. If the guide
is wrong and we leave it, we are discouraging researchers from applying in the language they write
best. Both failure modes are expensive and neither is a style question.

**Recommendation:** confirm directly with FRQ or with someone who has filed recently, then state it
plainly either way. **Do not ship the voice version's wording on this row without that check** —
note that the voice version keeps the *current* text precisely because of this.

**Confidence: Medium**, and the stakes are high enough that medium isn't good enough.

**Answered 2026-09-22 — the current text is wrong.** FRQ presentation standards: "The pre-application
and application forms must be filled out in French. The documents attached to the form can be written
in either French or English." The CV-FRQ instructions (July 2026, the authoritative French text) set
a limit for an English CV: "Le CV descriptif ne doit pas excéder 6 pages (ou 5 pages en version
anglaise)." The page now reads "French or English. The FRQ application form itself must be completed
in French." (P-32).

**One case still worth confirming with the FRQ:** on the new FRQnet portal the CV is completed
directly in the "CV-FRQ" section of the application instead of being attached. The instructions keep
the English page limit without distinguishing the two portals, which indicates English is accepted
there too, but the standards' wording (forms in French, attachments in either language) does not
settle that case outright.

### F-3 · Page limits are vaguer than what's known

**Current** (`s3.table`): `Varies by competition — always check the program guide` for both columns.

**Issue.** The limits are known and stable: **TCV is 5 pages in English, 6 in French**, with no
per-section limits. The CV-FRQ mirrors it at 5 English / 6 French. The genuine variability is
NSERC, which defers formatting to each funding opportunity — so "always check" is right for NSERC
and unhelpfully vague for everyone else.

**Also missing, and worth more than the limit itself:** overflow pages are removed **without
notification, before reviewers see the file** (TCV FAQ and reviewer guidelines). A researcher who
runs long doesn't get a warning — they get silently truncated. That belongs in a guide like this.

**Proposed:**
> `Length | 5 pages in English, 6 in French. NSERC sets formatting per opportunity — check yours |
> 5 pages in English, 6 in French`

plus a callout on the overflow rule.

**Confidence: High** for the page numbers and the overflow rule.

## Medium priority

### F-4 · CV-FRQ hyperlink rule stated too loosely

**Current:** `Permitted for supporting materials`

**Source:** permitted as bibliographic references where appropriate, **but the application must not
depend on external content**. "Supporting materials" reads as broader permission than that.

**Proposed:** "Permitted as bibliographic references, but your application must not depend on
external content."

**Confidence: Medium-high.**

### F-5 · The history can't be verified, and the rollout state is stale

**Current** (`s1.expand.p2`):
> "In Canada, the Tri-agency Narrative CV was introduced in 2021, piloted with select programs, and
> has since expanded."

**Issue.** I could not verify the 2021 date in either direction. Separately, "has since expanded" is
softer than the current picture, which is documented and dated:

- CIHR (2026-03-19): the TCV will **not** be used for the Project Grant before **Fall 2027 at the
  earliest**; 18 CIHR competitions piloted.
- SSHRC currently requires it in Destination Horizon, Knowledge Synthesis and Impact Awards.
- Concordia signed DORA in December 2024.

A researcher reading "has since expanded" may assume it applies to their competition when it
doesn't. Naming which competitions actually use it is more useful than the origin date.

**Recommendation:** either source the 2021 claim or drop it, and replace the expansion sentence with
the current rollout state. Note this section will need re-checking periodically — consider dating it.

**Confidence: Unverified** on 2021; **High** on the rollout facts.

### F-6 · "3–10 most significant contributions"

**Current** (`s4.table`, Scope row): `Curated selection (3–10 most significant contributions)`

**Issue.** The **cap of ten** is certain. The **floor of three** is not sourced anywhere I can find.
If it's workshop advice ("fewer than three looks thin"), it's good advice but should read as advice
rather than as a format rule sitting in a comparison table.

Note `s2.cards` already says "Up to 10", so the two slots disagree in implication.

**Confidence: Medium** — cap high, floor unsourced.

---

### F-7 · Missing: a cluster of work can count as one contribution

**Issue.** The TCV instructions state that a contribution need not be a single publication — a
cluster of related work counts as one. Carleton's guidance sharpens it: *avoid "one publication =
one contribution."*

This is genuinely load-bearing. A researcher with 40 papers and ten slots will otherwise assume they
must pick ten papers, when they could group them thematically and say something much stronger.

**Proposed:** add to `s2.expand-a.intro` — "…and a cluster of related work can count as a single
contribution."

**Confidence: High.**

---

### F-8 · Missing: reviewers are instructed to disregard JIF and h-index

**Issue.** `s4.table` lists "Citation counts, journal rankings, h-index" as the traditional-CV
column, which implies they're merely old-fashioned. The reviewer guidelines go further: JIF,
h-index, venue prestige, publication and citation counts *in isolation*, and grant size are **banned
as surrogates**, and reviewers are told to disregard them.

Concordia's own NCV FAQ already draws the finer line: an individual article's citation count is
defensible under DORA when it supports a qualitative claim; career-total counts, h-index and JIF are
not. That nuance is on our own website and absent from this guide.

**Confidence: High.**

---

### F-9 · "From passive to active voice" frames the shift as grammatical

**Current** (`s4.intro`): "…move from listing to explaining, and from passive to active voice."

**Issue.** Related to F-1. Active voice is a *means*; the requirement is that your role is visible.
A researcher could write entirely in the active voice and still hide their role ("the team
developed…"). Suggest reframing to "…and to make your own role visible in every entry."

**Confidence: Medium** — a framing judgment more than a factual error.

### F-10 · "Consistently score higher in reviewed contributions"

**Current** (`s6.lead`): "Four things that consistently score higher in reviewed contributions:"

**Issue.** This reads as a claim grounded in review outcome data. If it comes from the reviewer
guidelines or from observed panel behaviour, it's strong and should cite the basis. If it's distilled
workshop experience, that's also fine but should read that way.

**You'll know the answer to this one; I don't.**

**Confidence: Unverified.**

## Low priority

### F-11 · Missing drafting-time expectation

Concordia's own NCV page already publishes: *"Allow time: it can take a day or more to get your
first draft ready."* Setting that expectation early is one of the most useful things a guide like
this can do, and it costs one sentence in `header.lead` or `cta.body`. **Confidence: High** — it's
our own published copy.

### F-12 · No contact anywhere in the guide

The guide never tells a stuck researcher who to ask. Concordia's house style across the research
section is to name an individual with their title ("contact Gabriel Sales, Senior Advisor, Research
Security") — measured at 3–9× the rate of the funding agencies.

`impact@concordia.ca` is the verified Pathways address. **Is there a named narrative-CV advisor
whose name should appear here?** I deliberately did not invent one.

### F-13 · Missing formatting rules that trip people up

None of these are in the guide, and all are documented:

- **TCV:** Arial 12 for CIHR and SSHRC, black, single spacing minimum, 2 cm margins, unprotected PDF
  ≤ 5 MB. NSERC defers to the opportunity.
- **CV-FRQ:** Times New Roman 12, 2 cm margins, name in header, title in footer, PDF ~2 MB, and
  filename rules (≤ 50 characters, no spaces or accents).
- **CV-FRQ:** acronyms and technical terms **must be defined** — a 2026 addition, and an official
  rule rather than a style preference.

Might belong in the tool rather than this guide — worth deciding where. **Confidence: High.**

### F-14 · Missing the HQP asterisk and lead-author bolding convention

TCV citation convention: an asterisk after each supervised HQP's name, and bold the lead author
where authorship order doesn't make it clear. CV-FRQ additionally wants your own name and named
co-researchers bolded. Small, mechanical, and routinely got wrong. **Confidence: High.**

**F-15 · Section names.** The guide's cards read "Personal Statement", "Most Significant
Contributions" and "Supervisory & Mentorship Activities", and the table gives the CV-FRQ name as
"Réalisations les plus significatives". The agencies' names are: TCV, "Personal statement", "Most
significant contributions and experiences", "Supervisory and mentorship activities"; CV-FRQ,
"Parcours et compétences de la personne candidate", "Contributions et expériences les plus
importantes", "Activités de supervision et de mentorat" (English: "The candidate's background and
skills", "Most significant contributions and experiences", "Supervisory and mentoring activities").
The CV-FRQ's first section is not called a personal statement, so the table row label "Personal
Statement focus" also needs a neutral label.

**F-16 · How contributions are chosen.** Current: "Up to 10 contributions that give the most
complete picture of your research. Not necessarily your most recent — your most representative."
and "what gives the most complete picture of your research and its effects?" Both instruction pages
read "Describe up to 10 important contributions or experiences that relate to your application."
Concordia's published NCV page gives the same advice: "which of your research activities are most
relevant to the present grant opportunity? A very significant contribution may not be relevant for
every application". The guide should tie selection to the application.

**F-17 · Wellcome Trust.** `ncv-genre-sources.md` §3 sources UKRI (R4RI) and NWO (evidence-based
CV). Wellcome does not appear there. Either source it or remove it from the list.

**F-18 · Two naming errors.** "Key documents driving this include … the Coalition for Advancing
Research Assessment (CoARA)": CoARA is a coalition, and its document is the Agreement on Reforming
Research Assessment. "the traditional CV-style Common CV" should read "the Canadian Common CV
(CCV)".

**F-19 · The invented example.** "co-designed a food security protocol with three urban Indigenous
organizations in Montréal, subsequently adopted by the City's housing strategy". On an official
Concordia page this reads as a real case. It names Indigenous organizations and the City, and a
food-security protocol is paired with a housing strategy. **Is there a real example, usable with
consent, that could replace it?**

**F-20 · One FRQ.** The FRQ was established on June 1, 2024; the FRQNT, FRQS and FRQSC were
regrouped into it and continue as sectors (Nature et technologies, Santé, Société et culture). The
Funders row should name the FRQ and its three sectors.

**F-22 · Fonts differ by agency, and NSERC is stricter than it publishes.** Raised by NSERC to Prem
on 2026-09-22 and added to the page the same day. Verified at source that day:

- **SSHRC** (TCV instructions): "12-point, Arial font in black type. You can apply different fonts and
  sizes only in tables, figures and legends. However, the text must be clear and readable when the page
  is displayed at its normal size of 100%. Do not use condensed fonts."
- **NSERC** (Research Portal and NSERC Online presentation and attachment standards): "Use 12-point
  Times New Roman font for all text", and "All content, including references (if applicable), must
  follow these formatting standards."
- **CV-FRQ** (FRQ presentation standards, 2025-09-23): "Font : Times New Roman (12 points)."

**The open question.** NSERC's published standards carry a footnote that contradicts what NSERC told
Prem: "Font and size rules do not apply to text within tables and figures. However, the font must be
clearly legible." NSERC's word to Prem is that the TCV allows no such exception. The page publishes the
stricter reading, since following it cannot make an application non-compliant either way. **Worth
getting NSERC's position in writing, and asking whether the published standards will be updated.**

This also closes part of F-13: formatting rules do belong in the guide, not only the builder.

**F-21 · TCV hyperlink rule. Answered 2026-09-22.** The rule lives in the reviewer guidelines,
not the applicant instructions, which is why the string search of the instructions and FAQ found
nothing. "Guidelines for reviewing the tri-agency CV", section 5, "Self-contained information" (CIHR,
`cihr-irsc.gc.ca/e/54339.html`; NSERC publishes the same text without the last clause): "The sole
exception for which other sources may be permitted is for hyperlinks, but only if they are used to
demonstrate audio/visual creative outputs, if applicable for specific funding opportunities." and
"However, there is no guarantee that hyperlinks will be accessed." The page cell now carries both
conditions (P-31).

**For the NSERC conversation (with F-22):** NSERC's Research Portal presentation standards say "Do not
include hyperlinks or bookmarks in your documents", with no exception, while NSERC's own reviewer
guidelines allow the audio/visual one. This is the second place NSERC's published rules disagree.
