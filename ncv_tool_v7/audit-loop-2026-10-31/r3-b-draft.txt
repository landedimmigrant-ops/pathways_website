PERSONAL STATEMENT  [first in the CV, written last in the tool]
============================================================
I am a public health postdoctoral fellow at a university in Montréal. My work is in health services research with northern communities.

For six years my research has asked why people flown out of northern communities for care are sent home with so little follow-up, and why one in five is flown out again. Three strands address it: a trial of nurse-led medication review, a co-designed home check run by local health workers, and a cost model of repeat transfers. All three treat the return home as part of the care. I came to this work from nursing in a northern clinic, where I saw the same patients return within weeks, and I moved into health services research to measure it. I hold a CIHR postdoctoral fellowship, led a stepped-wedge trial in nine clinics, and the partner organization now runs the home check for 90 people a year. A Project Grant funds work built with the health authority that will use it, which is how this program has been run since 2021. The next phase takes the home check to three more nations and tests whether it lowers repeat evacuations over two years.

1. A rural medication-review trial that cut repeat transfers
   Patients on five or more medicines in rural clinics were often readmitted within a month because nobody reviewed their list after discharge. I designed the trial, wrote the protocol and supervised the pharmacists who ran the reviews. We enrolled 412 adults across nine rural clinics and followed them for 18 months; a pharmacist reviewed each patient's medicines within a week of discharge. A trial report, a one-page review checklist and an open dataset of 412 patient records. Repeat transfers fell by 14%, and two health authorities now fund the review.
