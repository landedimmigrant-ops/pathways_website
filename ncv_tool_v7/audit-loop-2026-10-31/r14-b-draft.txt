I am a postdoctoral fellow at Concordia University. My work is in developmental psychology, specifically infant sleep and parent stress.

How early stress shapes development: what happens inside the cell, and what happens to sleep and stress in new parents and infants at home. Two strands: stress hormones and neuron growth in cell culture, and sleep and stress in parents of infants. The thread is the first year of life, when stress has lasting effects. MOST SIGNIFICANT CONTRIBUTIONS AND EXPERIENCES
============================================================
1. Stress hormones and early neuron growth in cell culture
   Children exposed to high maternal stress hormones in pregnancy show altered stress responses, and the cellular route from hormone to altered neuron growth was not known. I ran the cell-culture experiments and wrote the first draft of the paper as first author, in a team of four. Over 14 months I grew cortical neurons from mice, exposed them to a stress hormone at three doses and measured how far their branches grew. I ran 48 culture batches. One first-author paper in a cell biology journal, and one conference talk at a neuroscience meeting. Two other students in the lab now use the culture protocol. If the dose-response pattern holds in human stem-cell neurons, other labs could use it to test how stress hormones affect early brain development. (a, b)
   a. Okafor-Reyes, A., Lindqvist, S., Chen, M. (2024). Dose-dependent glucocorticoid effects on neurite outgrowth in cultured cortical neurons. Journal of Cellular Neuroscience, 18(3), 211-224.  [Peer-reviewed]
      First-author paper from my PhD; the culture protocol is in the methods section.
   b. Okafor-Reyes, A. (2023). Glucocorticoid dose and neurite outgrowth in cultured cortical neurons [Conference talk]. Annual Meeting of the Society for Neural Development, Montreal.  [Conference talk]
      Selected for an oral session.

2. Infant sleep and parent stress in the first year
   Parents of infants report poor sleep and high stress in the first year, but few studies follow both together at home, so it is unclear which comes first. I co-designed the sleep protocol with my supervisor, analysed the first-year data and wrote the preprint as first author. We followed 64 families for 12 months with weekly sleep diaries and wrist sensors. I cleaned and analysed the data with two co-supervised undergraduates. The project produced one preprint under review, a cleaned dataset of 64 families that the lab now holds, and two conference talks, one given by me. If the preprint findings hold, home sleep support for parents could be tested in early-years clinics, and sleep could be tracked alongside parent mental health in public health follow-up. (a, b, c)
   a. Okafor-Reyes, A., Haddad, L., Tremblay, J. (2026). Weekly sleep and parent stress across the first year: a 64-family cohort [Preprint, under review]. PsyArXiv.  [Peer-reviewed]
      Preprint, not yet peer reviewed. First-year findings for the full cohort.
