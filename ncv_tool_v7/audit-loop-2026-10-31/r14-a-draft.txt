PERSONAL STATEMENT  [first in the CV, written last in the tool]
============================================================
I am an associate professor at a mid-sized Canadian university. My work is in human geography and flood-risk governance.

For 12 years my research has asked why flood maps leave out what river communities already know, and who is harmed when planning rests on the partial picture. Three strands: participatory mapping with residents, flood-history archives held by Indigenous partners, and tools that municipal engineers use; the thread is that communities set what counts as evidence. I trained in hydrology, moved to participatory methods after a 2013 flood in a river town, and began working under the direction of a First Nation planning office in 2018. Two prior SSHRC grants, a community flood atlas used by three municipalities, and an award from a provincial emergency management association. An Insight Grant fits because the question needs sustained work across basins and a team that includes community researchers. Next I will test whether the archive and mapping toolkit hold up in two Prairie river basins, with partners who set the questions.

1. A community-held archive of flood memory
   Elders knowledge of past floods is held in interviews and photographs that are not part of any official flood record, and it is at risk of being lost. I lead the archive with an elders council, who decide what is shared and what stays restricted. Recorded interviews with 24 elders and digitized family photographs across two river communities, 2022 to 2025. A searchable archive with community access controls, and a short documentary shown at three community gatherings. Two communities now use the archive in school programs, and the council used it in a 2025 land-use submission. (a, b)

2. Mapping flood risk with Indigenous and municipal partners
   Flood maps used in emergency planning often leave out the places that residents and Indigenous partners know flood first, so planning decisions rest on partial knowledge. The First Nation planning office and the municipal engineers set the questions and the data rules; I co-lead the mapping design and the participatory workshops. Participatory mapping workshops with residents, a flood-history archive built from elders interviews, and hydrological modelling, 2021 to 2025. A community flood atlas, two peer-reviewed articles and an open-source mapping toolkit used by three municipalities. The municipality revised its flood-response plan in 2024 and added three evacuation routes the maps identified. Other First Nations and municipalities in flood-prone river basins could adopt the toolkit, and provincial emergency management offices decide whether it enters standard practice. (a)
