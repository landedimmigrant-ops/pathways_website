# Pathways Voice — Register Corpus

*Sourced evidence for the `pathways-voice` skill: what Concordia actually writes, what Canadian
Press actually requires, and what the Tri-agency actually calls things. Compiled from a web scan
run 2026-08-10. Dates shown are the sources' own stamps.*

**How to read this:** §1–§3 are evidence with sources — verbatim quotations, not paraphrase.
§4 is the cross-register analysis that feeds the register system. §5 lists live defects found in
published Concordia copy. §6 lists what could NOT be verified — treat nothing there as
established. Where this corpus and the skill disagree, this corpus wins; where this corpus and a
funder's current instructions disagree, the funder wins.

Companion to `ncv-genre-sources.md` (which covers narrative-CV *structure* and hard formatting
rules). This document covers *language*. Deliberately no overlap.

---

## 1. Concordia — two registers, one domain

The critical finding of this scan: **Concordia does not have one voice. It has two**, and they
appear on adjacent pages within the same initiative. Naming them is the foundation of the whole
system.

### 1a. The institutional register ("Cabinet")

Primary sources:

| Document | URL | Date |
|---|---|---|
| Pathways to Impact (official page) | https://www.concordia.ca/research/for-researchers/pathways-to-impact.html | scanned 2026-08-10 |
| Concordia signs DORA (news) | https://www.concordia.ca/cunews/main/stories/2024/12/17/concordia-signs-the-san-francisco-declaration-on-research-assessment.html | 2024-12-17 |
| Research Impact Awards | https://www.concordia.ca/research/funding/awards/research-impact-awards.html | scanned 2026-08-10 |

Verbatim specimens:

> "To support our community in maximizing their contributions to science and society, we're
> developing **Pathways to Impact**, an initiative that leverages both existing and enhanced
> resources, tools and expertise to provide researchers and research institutes/teams with an
> **integrated support service**, centered around established impact pathways."

> "Pathways to Impact recognizes that impact requires **institutional support**. It must be
> enabled, facilitated, recognized and rewarded."

> "Promote collaboration and enhanced coordination across university units and with external
> partners to cultivate a system-wide cultural shift that makes achieving research impact easy,
> normative and rewarding."

> "…through a carefully curated web-based platform, launching soon."

Observable mechanics:

- **Nominalization as subject.** The actor is an abstraction: *collaboration*, *coordination*,
  *recognition*, *alignment*, *the initiative*. Humans appear as objects of support, not agents.
- **Agentless passive for obligations.** "It must be enabled, facilitated, recognized and
  rewarded" — four passives, no agent. Nobody is named as responsible.
- **Adjective stacking before institutional nouns.** *seamless integrated support service*,
  *carefully curated web-based platform*, *system-wide cultural shift*.
- **Triads and tetrads.** "resources, tools and expertise"; "enabled, facilitated, recognized and
  rewarded"; "easy, normative and rewarding"; "defined, funded and evaluated".
- **Aspirational tense.** "we're developing", "launching soon", "aims to".
- **Sentence length.** The three specimens above run 44, 21 and 28 words. The single longest
  sentence on the page is 47 words.

This register is legitimate and load-bearing. It is how an initiative describes itself to a
provost, a funder, a board, or a partner institution. It fails only when pointed at a researcher
who wants to know what to do on Tuesday.

### 1b. The practitioner register ("Bench")

Primary sources:

| Document | URL | Date |
|---|---|---|
| Narrative CV | https://www.concordia.ca/research/for-researchers/narrative-cv.html | scanned 2026-08-10 |
| Narrative CV FAQ | https://www.concordia.ca/research/for-researchers/narrative-cv/faq.html | states "At the time of writing (April 2025)" |
| Resources for researchers | https://www.concordia.ca/research/for-researchers.html | scanned 2026-08-10 |

Verbatim specimens:

> "Allow time: it can take a day or more to get your first draft ready"

> "Read the reviewer evaluation guidelines to get to know the criteria. Don't only read the
> instructions for applicants (though do read those too!)"

> "…don't try to relate everything – choose what's important for the proposed research."

> "It is important to be selective though: a chronological order does not mean you should discuss
> every experience you've had (you should not!)."

> "This is a huge question, and one we're giving separate focus to at Concordia. We don't have an
> answer for this question yet…"

> "Net negative: the writing generally isn't compelling. It's flat in intangible but unmistakable
> ways, no matter how much you tell the AI to 'make it more compelling'."

> "While neither the Tri-agency nor the FRQ currently prohibits noting the JIF or other
> journal-based metrics in their narrative CVs, it seems prudent to be reflective about using
> them."

Observable mechanics:

- **Second person, imperative mood, verb first.** *Allow. Know. Read. Think. Collect.*
- **Verbs the reader can actually perform.** Not *leverage* or *maximize* — *allow time*, *read
  the guidelines*, *collect evidence*.
- **Concrete quantities.** "a day or more", "four to six pages", "half a page".
- **Licensed informality.** Parenthetical asides ("you should not!"), contractions, an exclamation
  mark used once for emphasis rather than enthusiasm.
- **Calibrated hedging.** "it seems prudent to be reflective about" — the hedge marks a genuinely
  open question, not a liability dodge.
- **Admitted ignorance.** "We don't have an answer for this question yet." An institutional page
  saying this is a deliberate trust move, and it is the single most un-PR sentence in the corpus.
- **Sourced authority.** Claims are attached to the agency text they come from, quoted and linked.
- **Sentence length.** The specimens run 13, 24, 11, 27 words. Shorter, and the long ones are long
  because they carry a qualification, not because they carry adjectives.

**This register — not the institutional one — is the target voice for Pathways researcher-facing
product surfaces.** It already exists, it is already published under Concordia's name, and it is
already the work of the Pathways team.

### 1c. The hybrid that fails: imperative-shaped PR

The "Impact Pathways" cards on the official Pathways page are written verb-first, and still read
as PR:

> "Celebrate and elevate experimental artistic and creative practices that prioritize process over
> product."

> "Maximize your research impact by fostering trust and co-creation with community partners."

> "Leverage research through business partnerships and manage intellectual property effectively."

> "Enhance your impact by fostering public scholarship and sharing ideas with broader audiences."

Compare the practitioner register's "Read the reviewer evaluation guidelines."

The grammatical mood is identical. The difference is **whether the verb names an action the reader
can start**. *Read* is performable. *Maximize*, *leverage*, *enhance*, *elevate* are outcomes
wearing an imperative costume. A reader cannot "maximize" on Tuesday morning.

> **This is the load-bearing distinction of the whole system.** Verb-first is necessary but not
> sufficient. The test is performability, not mood. See `voice-checklist.md` → the Tuesday Test.

For contrast, the same site's own navigation labels pass the test cleanly: "Find a funding
opportunity", "Develop your proposal", "Submit your funding application", "Disclosing your
invention" (gerund — see §5.3).

## 2. Canadian Press style

Primary sources:

| Document | URL | Date |
|---|---|---|
| CP Stylebook (publisher description, 20th ed.) | https://www.thecanadianpress.com/writing-guides/the-canadian-press-stylebook/ | scanned 2026-08-10 |
| CP Style summary — Wilfrid Laurier | https://students.wlu.ca/academics/support-and-advising/student-success/assets/resources/writing/canadian-press-style.html | scanned 2026-08-10 |
| CP cheat sheet — New Canadian Media | https://newcanadianmedia.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/The-Canadian-Press-Stylebook_-Cheat-sheet-for-NCM-writers-1.pdf | 2021-05 |

The Stylebook itself is a paid product; the rules below come from the publisher's own description
and from published institutional summaries. **The Stylebook is not quoted at length here and has
not been purchased — where a specific ruling matters, check the current edition.** (§6.)

### 2a. Rules that bite on Pathways copy

| Domain | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Spelling | Canadian, per *Canadian Oxford* | colour, favour, rigour, behaviour, centre, metre, enrol, manoeuvre |
| Spelling | but *-ize/-yze*, not *-ise/-yse* | analyze, organize, recognize |
| Numbers | zero–nine spelled out; 10+ numerals | "nine pathways", "10 researchers" |
| Numbers | spell out at sentence start | "Twelve thousand four hundred and twenty-two people voted" |
| Numbers | *per cent*, two words, not % in prose | "75 per cent of buildings" |
| Punctuation | **no Oxford comma** | "two journal articles, one government report and three books" |
| Punctuation | one space after a period | — |
| Quotations | double quotes; single only for nested | — |
| Attribution | comma inside the closing quotation mark | — |
| Titles | capitalized directly before a name; lowercase otherwise | "President Graham Carr" / "the president said" |
| Titles | lowercase after *former*, *acting* | "former mayor Joanne Lesniak" |
| Dates | "the 1940s", "the '20s" | no apostrophe before the *s* |
| Capitalization | **Indigenous** capitalized | — |
| Capitalization | **Black** capitalized in racial contexts | — |
| Style | short, direct sentences of one or two clauses | — |
| Style | plain everyday words over formal language and jargon | — |

### 2b. Indigenous terminology

CP capitalizes *Indigenous*. Beyond capitalization, the consistent guidance across CP and the
Canadian institutional style guides scanned is: **name the specific nation, territory or people
rather than the pan-Indigenous term wherever the specific one is known**, and use the terms people
use for themselves. *Native*, *Indian*, *tribe* and *band* are not neutral defaults.

Where CP style and Indigenous style conflict, the widely-followed Canadian editorial convention —
and the one consistent with Pathways' decolonization and indigenization pillar — is to defer to
Indigenous style. Gregory Younging's *Elements of Indigenous Style* is the standard reference.
(Not independently verified against the current CP edition — see §6.)

### 2c. Where CP does not govern

CP is a **news** style. Three of its instincts must be overridden for this project:

1. **CP writes about people in the third person; product UI writes to the reader in the second.**
   CP's attribution machinery ("the researcher said") is irrelevant to a button label.
2. **CP's brevity is a wire-service constraint.** Pathways copy inherits CP's preference for short
   clauses, but a genuinely qualified statement is allowed to be long. The FAQ's 27-word
   qualifying sentences are correct writing, not CP violations.
3. **CP has no theory of interface text.** Nothing in CP governs button labels, empty states,
   error messages, or form help. Those need a UX-copy discipline CP does not supply.

CP's actual value here is as a **mechanical arbiter**: spelling, numbers, capitalization,
punctuation. Adopt it for those, and stop.

## 3. Tri-agency, CRC and FRQ — the vocabulary researchers recognize

### 3a. CIHR has migrated KT → KM (2026)

This is the single most consequential terminology finding of the scan, and it invalidates the
common assumption that *CIHR says knowledge translation, SSHRC says knowledge mobilization*.

| Document | URL | Date |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge Mobilization at CIHR | https://cihr-irsc.gc.ca/e/29529.html | modified 2026-05-28 |
| KM Strategy and Action Plan | https://cihr-irsc.gc.ca/e/54044.html | Cat. No. MR4-194/1-2026E-PDF |
| CIHR KM Glossary | https://cihr-irsc.gc.ca/e/54014.html | modified 2026-03-12 |

> "This knowledge mobilization (KM) component of the agency's mandate is an essential pathway for
> moving excellent research to impact."

> "This Strategy positions CIHR to enhance its global leadership in this area, building on over
> two decades of success in advancing knowledge translation (KT) practice and science."

KT is now framed as the **predecessor** practice. And the glossary retires *integrated knowledge
translation* explicitly:

> **Research co-production** — "Research approach that meaningfully engages potential knowledge
> users and/or knowledge holders as equal partners in the research process… **Formerly known at
> CIHR as integrated knowledge translation.**"

Practical consequence: **iKT is a legacy term.** Copy that offers to help researchers "plan their
integrated knowledge translation" is now describing a superseded CIHR concept.

Other CIHR glossary definitions worth holding verbatim:

> **Knowledge mobilization** — "The process of moving different types of knowledge into policy,
> practice and the public sphere, with the goal of improving health, supporting more effective
> health services and products, and strengthening health systems."

> **Knowledge user** — "An individual who is able to use the knowledge generated through research
> in order to make informed decisions about health policies, programs, practices and/or
> behaviors." *(Note: CIHR spells* behaviors *American here — see §5.4.)*

> **Knowledge holder** — "An individual with traditional or cultural knowledge or expertise.
> **(Source: Concordia University)**"

> **Open science** — "An approach to research that emphasizes accessible and equitable sharing of
> research outputs, resources, methods or tools, at any stage of the research process."

Also in the glossary and usable as-is: *knowledge brokering*, *knowledge sharing*, *knowledge
synthesis*, *community-led research*, *embedded research*, *de-implementation*, *diffusion*,
*dissemination*, *implementation*, *scaling*, *spreading*, *sustaining knowledge use*,
*commercialization*, *Indigenous-led solutions-focused health and wellness research*.

**Concordia is a cited source in a federal agency glossary.** That is an authority anchor worth
knowing about and worth not squandering with loose usage of the surrounding terms.

### 3b. SSHRC

| Document | URL | Date |
|---|---|---|
| SSHRC terminology / definitions | http://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/en/funding/terminology.aspx | scanned 2026-08-10 |

> **Knowledge mobilization** — "The reciprocal and complementary flow and uptake of research
> knowledge between researchers, knowledge brokers and knowledge users—both within and beyond
> academia."

SSHRC requires KMb to address at least one of: *within academia* — informing or advancing research
agendas, theory, methods; *beyond academia* — informing public debate, policies, practice,
services, and decisions in business, government, media and civil society.

> **Research-creation** — "An approach to research that combines creative and academic research
> practices, and supports the development of knowledge and innovation through artistic expression,
> scholarly investigation, and experimentation."

> **Formal partnership** — "A bilateral or multilateral formal collaboration agreement between an
> applicant and one or more partner organizations."

*Emerging scholar* is SSHRC's term for the early-career category; the definition turns on degree
completion within six years, with allowance for career interruptions.

**Convergence note:** SSHRC and CIHR now both use *knowledge mobilization* as the umbrella term.
Their definitions differ in emphasis — SSHRC's is **reciprocal flow** (bidirectional, brokered),
CIHR's is **moving knowledge into policy/practice** (directional, outcome-framed) — but the term
itself is now shared. This is new as of 2026 and simplifies the vocabulary problem considerably.

### 3c. NSERC

NSERC's public program pages scanned do not address the reader in the second person and did not
surface *highly qualified personnel* on the pages checked. HQP remains standard NSERC-adjacent
vocabulary in Canadian research administration, but **it was not verified on NSERC's own current
pages in this scan** — see §6.

The Tri-agency CV instructions do use HQP conventions (asterisk after each supervised HQP's name);
that is documented in `ncv-genre-sources.md` §1.

### 3d. Canada Research Chairs

| Document | URL | Date |
|---|---|---|
| CRC equity, diversity and inclusion | https://www.chairs-chaires.gc.ca/program-programme/equity-equite/index-eng.aspx | scanned 2026-08-10 |

The designated groups, in the program's own wording:

> "women and gender-equity seeking groups, persons with disabilities, Indigenous Peoples and
> racialized minorities"

On career interruptions:

> "The time devoted to these types of leaves must not be included in the assessment of their
> research productivity."

— referring to "career interruptions resulting from maternity, parental, medical or family

Note **"Indigenous Peoples"** capitalized, both words, in federal program text. Note also
*racialized minorities* rather than *visible minorities* in this page's phrasing, though the
*Employment Equity Act* term *visible minority* still appears in federal self-identification
instruments.

### 3e. The banned-metrics rule

Established in `ncv-genre-sources.md` §1 from the Tri-agency reviewer guidelines, and reinforced
by Concordia's own FAQ:

> "Peer-reviewers and selection committee members for Tri-agency applications are asked to
> disregard them." *(the JIF and other journal-based metrics)*

Concordia's FAQ draws the distinction precisely — individual-article citation counts are
defensible under DORA when used to support qualitative discussion; **career-total citation counts,
h-index, and Journal Impact Factor are not.**

This is not a style preference. Copy that invites researchers to showcase their h-index is
advising them toward something reviewers are instructed to ignore.

### 3f. Federal inclusive-language signals

Two observed conventions worth adopting:

- **"People living in Canada"**, not "Canadians" — CIHR's 2026 Strategy opens "People living in
  Canada face many health challenges". Avoids conflating residency with citizenship.
- **Land affirmation as a document element.** CIHR's Strategy carries a formal Land Affirmation
  naming the Algonquin Anishnaabeg Nation and acknowledging "First Nations, Inuit and Métis
  Peoples from coast to coast to coast."

## 4. Cross-register analysis

### 4a. What all three registers agree on

Remarkably, the three sources converge on more than they diverge:

| Principle | Concordia (Bench) | Canadian Press | Tri-agency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plain words over jargon | "Allow time" | explicit rule | plain-language application guides |
| Evidence attached to claims | "backed up by evidence" | attribution required | "impact, significance… and your role" |
| Named agent, no hiding | "your role" | active voice | role must be unambiguous |
| Metrics used honestly | JIF guidance | — | JIF/h-index disregarded |
| Specific over pan-category | — | name the nation | name the knowledge user |

The institutional register is the outlier on every row. That is the finding: **Pathways' own PR
voice is the only one of the four sources that prefers abstraction.**

### 4b. Where they genuinely conflict

1. **Sentence length.** CP wants one-or-two clauses. Tri-agency narrative CV guidance rewards
   qualified, contextualized statements. → Resolution: CP governs *interface and explanatory* copy;
   qualification is allowed to run long in *guidance* copy where the qualification is the content.
2. **Second person.** CP is third-person news style; the product speaks to "you". → CP does not
   govern person. Use second person.
3. **Terminology density.** Plain language says avoid jargon; researchers recognize and *trust*
   the controlled vocabulary. → Resolution: agency terms are not jargon **to the audience that
   uses them**. Use the exact agency term, gloss it once on first use, never invent a synonym.
   Inventing a friendlier synonym for a controlled term is the worse error.
4. **"Impact".** Institutionally a banner word; to a researcher, a specific claim requiring
   evidence. → Never use "impact" as a bare intensifier.

### 4c. The two axes

The scan supports a two-axis model rather than a single formality dial:

- **Axis 1 — Abstraction.** Is the subject of the sentence a person or an abstraction?
- **Axis 2 — Performability.** Can the reader start the named action today?

The institutional register sits high-abstraction / low-performability. The practitioner register
sits low-abstraction / high-performability. The failed hybrid in §1c sits low-abstraction in mood
but low-performability in fact — which is why verb-first alone does not rescue it.

The system built in `SKILL.md` operationalizes these two axes.

## 5. Live defects in published Concordia copy

Found during the scan. Recorded as evidence that the checks in this skill catch real things —
**not** as a work order. Nothing here has been reported or changed.

1. **Inconsistent capitalization of "Anti-Black Racism".** The Pathways page lists
   "anti-black racism" (lowercase) in the Objectives section and "**Anti-Black Racism**"
   (capitalized) in the Key Pillars section, on the same page. CP style capitalizes *Black*.
   The lowercase instance is wrong by both CP and internal consistency.
2. **Inconsistent Canadian spelling of "centre".** The Pathways page has "**centered** around
   established impact pathways" (US); the DORA news story has "**centred** around diverse avenues"
   (Canadian). CP requires the Canadian form. Separately, *centred on* is preferable to *centred
   around* in careful usage.
3. **Mixed gerund/imperative in a parallel link list.** "Resources for researchers" runs
   "Find a funding opportunity", "Develop your proposal", "Submit your funding application" and
   then "**Disclosing** your invention" — one gerund in an otherwise imperative series.
4. **"behaviors" (US) in CIHR's own glossary** — noted so that quoting CIHR verbatim does not get
   flagged as a Pathways spelling error. Quote it as written; do not silently Canadianize inside
   quotation marks.
5. **Typo in the Key Pillars section:** "tacking global challenges" for *tackling*.
6. **"launching soon"** on a page for a platform under development since a 2021 proposal. Undated
   futurity ages badly; it is the kind of phrase a voice check should flag for a date or removal.
7. **The Oxford comma is inconsistent within a single page.** The Pathways page carries "how
   research is being **designed, funded, and assessed**" (serial comma) a few paragraphs from "how
   research is **defined, funded and evaluated**" (none) — two near-identical constructions about
   the same idea. CP forbids the serial comma; either ruling is survivable, the inconsistency is
   not.

### 5b. Live prototype copy — audit, 2026-08-10

Counted programmatically across ~2,800 words of prose strings in `data.js`. Recorded as evidence
that the checks catch real things. **Nothing was changed.**

| Finding | Count | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Serial (Oxford) commas | **61** | T6 — CP forbids; also inconsistent with Concordia's dominant published usage |
| US spellings | **1** — `behavior`, `data.js:611` | T6 |
| Abstraction markers (*leverage, seamless, holistic, robust*…) | **1** — `ecosystem` | T1/T2 |
| `%` used in prose | 0 | T6 — clean |
| Bare digits 0–9 | 2, both legitimate (an outcome label, a numbered objective) | T6 — clean |
| `impact*` | 82 in ~2,800 words (~29 per 1,000) | T7 — inflated legitimately by the project's name; flag only bare-modifier uses |

Two non-findings worth recording so they are not "fixed" later: **`Erasmus Medical Center`**
(`data.js:960`) is a proper noun, and **`program`** is correct Canadian spelling — *programme* would
be the error.

**The prototype's copy scores better on abstraction than Concordia's own official Pathways page.**
One abstraction marker across 2,800 words is a good result. The live gaps are overwhelmingly
mechanical, not tonal.

## 6. Not verified — do not treat as established

- **The CP Stylebook itself was not consulted.** It is a paid product. All CP rules above come
  from the publisher's public description plus institutional summaries (Wilfrid Laurier, New
  Canadian Media). The rules cited are stable and widely reported, but **any edge case must be
  checked against the current edition before it is treated as binding.** The NCM cheat-sheet PDF
  would not extract as text; the Centennial College open guide returned HTTP 403.
- **CP's current ruling on Indigenous style precedence** — the "defer to Indigenous style"
  convention is well attested across Canadian institutional guides but was not confirmed in CP's
  own text.
- **NSERC and HQP** — not found on the current NSERC pages scanned. Do not assert that NSERC
  requires or prefers the term.
- **Concordia has no public institutional editorial style guide.** Searches surfaced only the
  unrelated US Concordia universities (Texas, Wisconsin, Nebraska, Irvine). If Concordia Montreal
  maintains an internal brand/editorial guide, it is not publicly indexed, and this corpus is
  therefore reconstructed from published pages rather than from a stated standard. **If such a
  guide exists internally, it supersedes §1 and §2 of this document.**
- **French / bilingual register.** Not scanned. Concordia publishes French equivalents
  (`Atelier-CV-descriptif-fr.pdf`, "Consultez la foire aux questions"), and the FRQ operates in
  French with the French instructions authoritative (`ncv-genre-sources.md` §2). The voice rules
  here are **English-only** and should not be assumed to transfer.
- **FRQ's own terminology page** was not scanned in this pass; FRQ structural rules are covered in
  `ncv-genre-sources.md` §2.
- Dates given as "scanned 2026-08-10" are retrieval dates, not publication dates; several
  Concordia pages carry no visible modification stamp.
